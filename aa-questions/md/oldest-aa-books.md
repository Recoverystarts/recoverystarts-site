# Which A.A. books are oldest, and why does the date matter?

**In short:**
- The oldest A.A. text is the Big Book, *Alcoholics Anonymous*, first published in April 1939. Its first 164 pages and the Foreword to the First Edition are the 1939 text.
- Next come Bill W.'s Grapevine essays on the Traditions (1946–47) and the Traditions' Long Form (1946). Almost everything else in our sources were written or compiled after Dr. Bob died in 1950 (the exceptions are pieces reprinted inside later books, such as Dr. Bob's 1948 talk in P-53): the 1955 forewords and appendix, *A.A. Comes of Age* (1957), the pamphlets, *Dr. Bob and the Good Oldtimers* (1980) and *Pass It On* (1984).
- The date matters because later accounts sometimes add details, change them, or disagree with the earlier text. When two sources differ, the earlier one is closer to the events, and the reader deserves to see both.
- A later book isn't wrong because it is later. It just has to be read with its date in view.

## Why the date matters: examples from these pages

Reading the sources side by side shows details shifting as the story was retold:
- **Bill D., A.A. number three.** His own story says he was hospitalized eight times in six months and came to on June 26, 1935 (p. 184). Bill W.'s 1957 account has him say six times in four months and puts the visit within days of June 10 (A.A. Comes of Age, 1957). See *Who was the third member, and how was he found?*
- **Where Dr. Bob trained.** P-53 and the 1980 biography put his internship at City Hospital, Akron; Bill's 1957 account says Chicago (A.A. Comes of Age, 1957). See *Who was Dr. Bob?*
- **The first meeting.** The 1955 foreword says June 1935 (p. xv); Dr. Bob's own 1939 story puts it on Mother's Day, weeks before June 10 (p. 179). See *How did Bill W. and Dr. Bob meet?*
- **Names appear later.** The 1939 text names no one in the founding story; the hotel, the minister and Henrietta Seiberling are named in 1948 and 1957 accounts. That isn't a contradiction: the 1939 book asked its members to stay anonymous in print (p. xiii), and the names came out later.

The later books are honest about this risk themselves. *Dr. Bob and the Good Oldtimers* quotes an oldtimer saying people are "always repeating things Dr. Bob said that I know he didn't" (Dr. Bob and the Good Oldtimers, 1980).

## How Recovery Starts uses dates
- The earliest source leads. Where the 1939 or 1955 Big Book covers a point, it is quoted first, and later books add detail with their dates attached.
- Every source on every page is dated, so a reader can weigh it.
- Where sources disagree, both are given with their dates, and the page says our sources don't settle it.
- Later literature is read with caution: not because it is wrong, but because many other factors were in play by the time it was written. The pages always come back to the early days.

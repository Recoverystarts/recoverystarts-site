Where are the Twelve Steps in the Big Book?

In short:

  • The Twelve Steps are printed in Chapter 5, "How It Works," on pages 59 and 60 of the Big Book. Steps 1 to 11 are on page 59; Step 12 runs onto page 60.
  • Chapter 5 opens on page 58 by saying the writers have rarely "seen a person fail who has thoroughly followed our path" (p. 58). The often-heard line "Rarely is a man constituted…" isn't in the book.
  • The list is only the summary. The book explains how the Steps are taken across Chapters 5, 6 and 7 (pp. 58–103), and Chapters 1 to 4 lay the ground for the first two.

The page the Steps are on

Chapter 5 starts on page 58. A few paragraphs in, after "Half measures availed us nothing" (p. 59), the book introduces the list with the words "Here are the steps we took, which are suggested as a program of recovery" (p. 59). Steps 1 to 11 follow on page 59, and Step 12 finishes on page 60.

The book itself refers back to this page as the place to find them. After the Fifth Step, the reader is told to take the book down and turn "to the page which contains the twelve steps" (p. 75).

Where each Step is explained

The list on pages 59–60 is followed by the chapters that say how each Step was done:

Steps Where the book explains them Pages
1 and 2 The chapters before: "There Is a Solution," "More About Alcoholism," "We Agnostics." Page 60 says those chapters and the stories make clear "That we were alcoholic and could not manage our own lives" (p. 60), and that God could help. 17–57, 60
3 Chapter 5, the decision and the prayer that goes with it 60–63
4 Chapter 5, the written inventory 63–71
5 to 11 Chapter 6, "Into Action" 72–88
12 Chapter 7, "Working With Others." Chapter 6 ends by saying the next chapter "is entirely devoted to Step Twelve" (p. 88). 89–103

About the chapter's opening line

Chapter 5's first sentence is the one quoted above, about people who have "thoroughly followed our path" (p. 58). A search of the whole text in our sources find no "Rarely is a man constituted." The page goes on to say that those who don't recover are usually people "constitutionally incapable of being honest with themselves" (p. 58), which is probably where the misquote's "constituted" comes from.

Before the Twelve Steps

The twelve were not the first form of the program. One of the early stories in the back of the book, "He Sold Himself Short," describes Akron before the Big Book was written, when Dr. Bob took a newcomer through "the Six-Step program as it was at that time" (p. 263). That is a later retelling of the early days, so it's a pointer, not a history; see How long did the first members take to work the Steps?

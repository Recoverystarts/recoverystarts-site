The Daily Reprieve on Page 85 of the Big Book

What maintaining our spiritual condition actually looks like, one day at a time

The words people remember are on page 85 of Alcoholics Anonymous, in the chapter called Into Action: 'What we really have is a daily reprieve contingent on the maintenance of our spiritual condition' (page 85). In plain language, sobriety is not a prize we win once and keep forever. It is renewed one day at a time, and it holds only as long as we keep doing the spiritual work that produced it. Two sentences earlier the book is blunt: 'We are not cured of alcoholism' (page 85). A reprieve is not a pardon. It is a stay, granted for today.

So what is the maintenance the reprieve depends on? The book does not leave us guessing. That sentence sits in the middle of the instructions for Steps Ten and Eleven (pages 84-88). Maintenance means a personal inventory that runs all day, mistakes set right quickly, and prayer and meditation in the morning, at night, and whenever we get rattled in between. The sections below walk through it straight from the text.

What Page 85 Is Warning Us About

Just before the famous line, the book describes how good recovered life can feel. The problem has been removed. We are not fighting liquor or even avoiding temptation, and we feel as if we had been placed in 'a position of neutrality—safe and protected' (page 85). That new attitude toward liquor arrives without any thought or effort on our part (page 85). Then comes the warning: 'It is easy to let up on the spiritual program of action and rest on our laurels. We are headed for trouble if we do, for alcohol is a subtle foe' (page 85). The threat is not that the program stops working. The threat is that we stop working the program because it worked. The neutrality lasts, the book says, 'so long as we keep in fit spiritual condition' (page 85).

Step Ten: The Inventory That Never Stops

Maintenance begins on page 84 with Step Ten, which asks us to keep taking personal inventory and to set right new mistakes as we go along (page 84). The instruction is concrete: 'Continue to watch for selfishness, dishonesty, resentment, and fear' (page 84). When these crop up, we ask God at once to remove them, talk them over with someone, and make amends quickly if we have harmed anyone, then turn our thoughts to someone we can help (page 84). This is not a yearly housecleaning. It is a spot-check we run in the middle of ordinary days, and the book says it should continue for our lifetime (page 84). The attitude behind it fits in seven words: 'Love and tolerance of others is our code' (page 84).

Step Eleven: Morning, Night, and All Through the Day

Page 85 then turns to Step Eleven, prayer and meditation, and pages 86 through 88 lay out a rhythm anyone can follow. At night: 'When we retire at night, we constructively review our day. Were we resentful, selfish, dishonest or afraid?' (page 86). After the review we ask God's forgiveness and what corrective measures should be taken, careful not to slide into worry or morbid reflection (page 86). In the morning: 'On awakening let us think about the twenty-four hours ahead' (page 86), asking God to direct our thinking before the day starts. And in the thick of things: 'As we go through the day we pause, when agitated or doubtful, and ask for the right thought or action' (page 87), reminding ourselves that we are no longer running the show (pages 87-88). The book closes these instructions with a plain reassurance: 'It works—it really does' (page 88).

Why a Daily Reprieve Is Good News

At first, contingent sobriety can sound like living under a suspended sentence. Read it again and it turns into freedom. We are not asked to stay sober forever. We are asked to maintain our spiritual condition today, and 'Every day is a day when we must carry the vision of God's will into all of our activities' (page 85). The book even calls this kind of effort the proper use of the will (page 85), so we are not passive in it. The maintenance is not a tax added on top of sobriety. It is the same set of actions that made us well, kept warm. Yes, the reprieve is contingent. It is also renewable every single morning, for as long as we are willing to do the simple things pages 84 through 88 describe. We do not have to be cured. We have something that works one day at a time, and today is the only day we are asked to work it.

— Alcoholics Anonymous (the Big Book), 4th Edition, chapter 'Into Action', pages 84-88; the daily reprieve sentence is on page 85

Quick answers

What page is the daily reprieve quote on in the Big Book?

Page 85 of Alcoholics Anonymous, 4th Edition, in the chapter Into Action. The full sentence reads: 'What we really have is a daily reprieve contingent on the maintenance of our spiritual condition' (page 85). It comes in the discussion of Step Ten, right after the book states, 'We are not cured of alcoholism' (page 85).

What does 'contingent on the maintenance of our spiritual condition' mean?

It means our sobriety depends on daily spiritual upkeep, not on time accumulated. The Big Book defines that upkeep as Step Ten's continuous personal inventory, watching for selfishness, dishonesty, resentment, and fear (page 84), and Step Eleven's prayer and meditation in the morning, at night, and whenever we are agitated or doubtful (pages 86-87). We keep doing today what got us well yesterday.

Does the Big Book say alcoholics can be cured?

No. Page 85 says plainly, 'We are not cured of alcoholism.' What we have instead is a daily reprieve that is renewed by maintaining our spiritual condition, and the book warns that alcohol is a subtle foe and that resting on our laurels leads to trouble (page 85).

How do I maintain my spiritual condition every day?

The Big Book gives a simple rhythm on pages 84 through 88. Through the day we watch for selfishness, dishonesty, resentment, and fear and set mistakes right quickly (page 84); on awakening we plan the day and ask God to direct our thinking (page 86); when agitated or doubtful we pause and ask for the right thought or action (page 87); and at night we constructively review our day (page 86).

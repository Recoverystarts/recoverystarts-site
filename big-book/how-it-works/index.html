How It Works: Chapter 5 of the Big Book

The words we hear at almost every meeting: pages 58 to 60, taken slowly

How It Works is the title of Chapter 5 of Alcoholics Anonymous, the Big Book, and the part most of us know by heart sits on pages 58 through 60. This is the passage read aloud at the start of almost every AA meeting. It opens with one of the most famous sentences in recovery: "Rarely have we seen a person fail who has thoroughly followed our path" (page 58). In three short pages the book hands us the whole program in miniature: honesty as the one requirement, willingness to go to any length, the Twelve Steps themselves, and three plain conclusions about our condition and where the help comes from.

So how does it work? Through honesty and thoroughness. Page 58 says the people who do not recover are usually those who "cannot or will not completely give themselves to this simple program," people who are "constitutionally incapable of being honest with themselves" (page 58). Notice what that sentence does not say. It does not say we fail because we are too weak, too far gone, or too bad. The book puts the outcome in our hands: if we can get honest with ourselves and follow the path thoroughly, we recover.

The Promise and the Price on Page 58

Page 58 asks whether we want what sober members have and whether we are willing to go to any length to get it. Many of us balked at some of the Steps. "We thought we could find an easier, softer way. But we could not" (page 58). So the book pleads with us: "With all the earnestness at our command, we beg of you to be fearless and thorough from the very start" (page 58). Holding on to our old ideas got us nowhere until we let go absolutely. Then comes the reminder of the opponent: alcohol is "cunning, baffling, powerful" (pages 58 and 59), and without help it is too much for us. But there is One who has all power, and the book's whole hope for us fits in five words: "May you find Him now!" (page 59).

Half Measures Availed Us Nothing

Page 59 gives us the hinge of the chapter: "Half measures availed us nothing. We stood at the turning point. We asked His protection and care with complete abandon" (page 59). Most of us tried the half measures first. We cut down, switched drinks, made promises, worked a Step or two and skipped the uncomfortable ones. The turning point is the moment we admit our own plans have run out and give ourselves to the program completely. Right after that surrender the book says, "Here are the steps we took, which are suggested as a program of recovery" (page 59), and lists all Twelve Steps across pages 59 and 60. Suggested, not commanded. The book never bullies us; it simply reports what worked for the people who wrote it.

The abc's on Page 60

After the Steps, the book gathers everything into three pertinent ideas, the ones we call the abc's (page 60): (a) "That we were alcoholic and could not manage our own lives," (b) "That probably no human power could have relieved our alcoholism," and (c) "That God could and would if He were sought" (page 60). The first is Step One in a single line. The second closes the exits we kept trying: willpower alone, the love of our families, our own best thinking. None of it relieved the illness by itself. The third is pure hope, and it is worth reading slowly. It does not say God might help us. It says He could and would, on one condition only: that He be sought.

Spiritual Progress, Not Perfection

Page 60 also expects our panic. Many of us exclaimed, "What an order! I can't go through with it." The reply is gentle: "Do not be discouraged" (page 60). The book admits that "No one among us has been able to maintain anything like perfect adherence to these principles" (page 60). "We are not saints. The point is, that we are willing to grow along spiritual lines" (page 60). Then comes the sentence many of us lean on every day: "We claim spiritual progress rather than spiritual perfection" (page 60). That is why these pages open our meetings. Not to scold us, but to remind us that the path is walkable. We show up honest, we take the Steps thoroughly, and we let God do for us what we could not do for ourselves.

— Alcoholics Anonymous (the Big Book), 4th Edition. Chapter 5, "How It Works," pages 58-71; passage discussed: pages 58-60 (the opening promise, the Twelve Steps, half measures, the abc's, spiritual progress not perfection).

Quick answers

What pages are 'How It Works' in the Big Book?

Chapter 5, 'How It Works,' begins on page 58 of the 4th Edition, and the portion read aloud at most AA meetings runs from page 58 through the abc's on page 60. The Twelve Steps are listed on pages 59 and 60. The full chapter continues to page 71, where it carries us into Step Four.

What are the abc's in the Big Book?

The abc's are the three pertinent ideas on page 60: (a) that we were alcoholic and could not manage our own lives, (b) that probably no human power could have relieved our alcoholism, and (c) that God could and would if He were sought. Together they compress the first two Steps into three lines. They come right after the Twelve Steps are listed and sum up everything the book has argued to that point.

What does 'half measures availed us nothing' mean?

On page 59 the Big Book says half measures availed us nothing, that we stood at the turning point, and that we asked God's protection and care with complete abandon. It means partial effort at the program got us nowhere; cutting down, bargaining, or working only the comfortable Steps did not relieve our alcoholism. The turning point is choosing to give ourselves to the program completely rather than keeping one foot in the old life.

Does 'rarely have we seen a person fail' mean AA never fails?

It is not a guarantee; it is a report of experience with a condition attached. Page 58 says those who do not recover are usually people who cannot or will not be completely honest with themselves, so the emphasis falls on honesty and thoroughness, not on being flawless. Page 60 balances it by saying we claim spiritual progress rather than spiritual perfection.

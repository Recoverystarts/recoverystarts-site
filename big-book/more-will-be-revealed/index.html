More Will Be Revealed: What Page 164 of the Big Book Means

The last page of the basic text promises that our understanding will keep growing

In recovery, 'more will be revealed' is shorthand for one of the most hopeful promises in the Big Book. It comes from page 164, the very last page of the basic text, where the first members wrote, 'Our book is meant to be suggestive only. We realize we know only a little. God will constantly disclose more to you and to us.' That is the whole idea in three plain sentences. The people who wrote the book were not handing us a finished system. They were handing us a beginning, and they promised that God would keep teaching all of us, writers and readers alike, as we walked forward.

The saying means two things at once. First, humility. Even the founders admitted they knew only a little, so none of us has to pretend we have it all figured out. Second, hope. Our understanding of God, of ourselves, and of this way of living is not stuck at today's level. If we stay sober and keep doing the work, more gets disclosed. The book calls itself suggestive rather than final (page 164), and that leaves room for every one of us to grow past where we started.

Where the Phrase Comes From

Here is a detail worth knowing: the exact words 'more will be revealed' do not appear anywhere in the Big Book. The fellowship condensed the page 164 promise into a saying, and it stuck because it names something we all experience. The passage sits in the closing paragraphs of the chapter A Vision For You, at the end of the basic text. Right after promising that God will constantly disclose more, the book gives us a job. It says, 'Ask Him in your morning meditation what you can do each day for the man who is still sick.' Then it adds, 'The answers will come, if your own house is in order.' Revelation and action travel together. We do not receive more light by sitting still. We receive it by praying, cleaning house, and helping the next alcoholic (page 164).

Why 'Suggestive Only' Matters

Calling the book suggestive was a deliberate act of humility, and it protects us from turning the program into a rigid rulebook. The same spirit shows up on page 60, where the book says, 'We claim spiritual progress rather than spiritual perfection.' The Steps are offered there as guides to progress, and the book admits that no one among us has maintained anything like perfect adherence to these principles (page 60). If the founders could say they knew only a little, we can say it too. That admission keeps us teachable, and teachable people stay sober.

It also matters because our reprieve is daily. Page 85 reminds us that we are not cured of alcoholism; what we really have is 'a daily reprieve contingent on the maintenance of our spiritual condition.' A daily reprieve calls for daily growth. If the book had claimed to be the final word, we might read it once and coast. Because it claims to be suggestive only, we keep coming back, to the text and to God, for the next lesson.

How More Gets Revealed in Practice

The disclosure is gradual, and the book says so. Describing life after Step Ten, page 84 tells us our next function is to grow in understanding and effectiveness, and that 'This is not an overnight matter. It should continue for our lifetime.' We work the Steps, we pray and meditate, we keep our side of the street clean, and understanding arrives on God's schedule rather than ours. Most of us can see it when we look back. Page 100 says we realize that 'the things which came to us when we put ourselves in God's hands were better than anything we could have planned.'

So when an old-timer smiles and says more will be revealed, they are not brushing us off. They are handing us the last page of the basic text, in spirit if not in letter. The God of our understanding is still teaching, the program we know today is not the ceiling, and our part is the willingness, honesty, and open-mindedness that Appendix II names as essentials (page 568). We show up for morning meditation, we keep our house in order, we give freely of what we find. The book closes by promising that 'you will surely meet some of us as you trudge the Road of Happy Destiny' (page 164). More will be revealed on that road. It always is.

— Alcoholics Anonymous (Big Book), 4th Edition: pages 60, 84-85, 100, 164, 568 (Appendix II)

Quick answers

Where does 'more will be revealed' come from in the Big Book?

The exact phrase does not appear verbatim in the Big Book. It grew out of page 164, the last page of the basic text, which says, 'Our book is meant to be suggestive only. We realize we know only a little. God will constantly disclose more to you and to us.' Members shortened that promise into the saying we hear at meetings.

What does 'more will be revealed' mean in AA?

It means our understanding keeps growing as long as we keep working the program. The founders admitted they knew only a little and promised that God would constantly disclose more (page 164). Since the book claims spiritual progress rather than spiritual perfection (page 60), nobody graduates; we all stay students.

What page is 'Our book is meant to be suggestive only' on?

It is on page 164 of Alcoholics Anonymous, 4th Edition. The passage sits in the closing paragraphs of the chapter A Vision For You, the final page of the basic text before the personal stories begin.

How do I practice 'more will be revealed' in my recovery?

Page 164 ties the promise directly to action: ask God in morning meditation what you can do for the alcoholic who still suffers, and keep your own house in order so the answers can come. Page 84 adds that growing in understanding is not an overnight matter but a lifetime practice, so we keep working the Steps one day at a time.

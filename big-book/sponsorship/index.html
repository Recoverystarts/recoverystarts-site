What Does the Big Book Say About Sponsorship?

Working With Others: how carrying the message became what we call sponsorship

The word 'sponsor' barely appears in the first 164 pages of the Big Book. That surprises a lot of us, because sponsorship feels like the backbone of AA life. But the practice itself is right there, laid out in detail in Chapter 7, 'Working With Others' (pages 89-103). The chapter opens with one of the most quoted promises in the book: 'Practical experience shows that nothing will so much insure immunity from drinking as intensive work with other alcoholics. It works when other activities fail' (page 89). That is sponsorship in a single sentence. One alcoholic who has recovered sits down with one who is still suffering and passes on what was freely given to him.

The practice flows straight out of Step Twelve: 'Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these steps, we tried to carry this message to alcoholics, and to practice these principles in all our affairs' (page 60). Chapter 7 names it plainly: 'This is our twelfth suggestion: Carry this message to other alcoholics!' (page 89). So the honest answer to our question is this: the Big Book never describes a title or an office. It describes an action. Sponsorship is simply the name the fellowship later gave to Step Twelve work done one alcoholic to another, over time and with love.

Why We Work With Others

The book is plain about why we do this: it keeps us sober. 'Helping others is the foundation stone of your recovery' (page 97). Earlier it goes even further: 'Our very lives, as ex-problem drinkers, depend upon our constant thought of others and how we may help meet their needs' (page 20). This is why sponsorship is never a favor the sponsor grants. When the book tells us to explain the program to a newcomer, it adds, 'Actually, he may be helping you more than you are helping him' (page 94). Chapter 6 closes with the line that sets all of this up: 'Faith without works is dead' (page 88). We stay well by giving away what we found.

How the Big Book Says to Sponsor Someone

Pages 89 through 96 read like a field manual. We are told to cooperate with doctors and ministers and never criticize, because 'To be helpful is our only aim' (page 89). We are told not to chase the unwilling: 'If he does not want to stop drinking, don't waste time trying to persuade him' (page 90). When the newcomer is ready to listen, our instructions are simple: 'Tell him exactly what happened to you. Stress the spiritual feature freely' (page 93). We share experience, not lectures. The book warns us to 'Never talk down to an alcoholic from any moral or spiritual hilltop; simply lay out the kit of spiritual tools for his inspection' (page 95). And it keeps us humble about our own answers: 'We have no monopoly on God; we merely have an approach that worked with us' (page 95).

A Sponsor Points to God, Not to Himself

Chapter 7 draws one boundary that every sponsor and every newcomer needs to hear. The relationship is a bridge, not a crutch. The book says 'we simply do not stop drinking so long as we place dependence upon other people ahead of dependence on God' (page 98). Then comes the strongest sentence in the chapter: 'Burn the idea into the consciousness of every man that he can get well regardless of anyone. The only condition is that he trust in God and clean house' (page 98). A good sponsor takes us through the Steps and then steps out of the way, pointing past himself to the Power that actually does the healing. Our job, the book says, is to 'be at the place where you may be of maximum helpfulness to others' (page 102).

So if we are new, we look for someone who has recovered through these Steps and ask them to take us through the book, page by page. If we have some sobriety, the message is even simpler: this work is not extra credit. It is the program's own protection plan, and it comes with a promise. Watching people recover, watching loneliness vanish, and watching a fellowship grow up around us is, the book says, 'an experience you must not miss' (page 89). Frequent contact with newcomers is called 'the bright spot of our lives' (page 89). We carry the message today, and the message carries us.

— Alcoholics Anonymous (Big Book), 4th Edition. Primary: Chapter 7, 'Working With Others,' pp. 89-103 (cited: 89, 90, 93, 94, 95, 97, 98, 102). Also: Step Twelve, p. 60 ('How It Works'); p. 88 ('Into Action'); p. 20 ('There Is a Solution').

Quick answers

Does the Big Book actually use the word sponsor?

Hardly at all in the basic text. What we now call sponsorship comes from Step Twelve (page 60) and Chapter 7, Working With Others (pages 89-103), which give detailed instructions for one alcoholic carrying the message to another. The word came later from AA practice; the action was there from the beginning.

What chapter of the Big Book is about sponsorship?

Chapter 7, Working With Others, pages 89 through 103. It opens by promising that nothing insures immunity from drinking like intensive work with other alcoholics (page 89), then walks through exactly how to approach, talk with, and guide a newcomer.

Do I need a sponsor to work the Steps according to the Big Book?

The Big Book never makes a sponsor a formal requirement, but its whole design assumes the Steps are passed from one alcoholic to another; that is what Chapter 7 describes (pages 89-103). It also reminds us the newcomer can get well regardless of anyone if he trusts God and cleans house (page 98). Most of us find that a sponsor is simply how the book's plan works in daily life.

What does the Big Book say a sponsor should do?

Tell the newcomer exactly what happened to them and stress the spiritual side (page 93), lay out the kit of spiritual tools without preaching from any hilltop (page 95), and point the newcomer to God rather than to themselves (page 98). The book says this work protects the sponsor's own sobriety too (pages 89 and 97).

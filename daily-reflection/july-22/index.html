July 22

"THE GOOD AND THE BAD"

Recovery Einstein's Big Book perspective on the AA Daily Reflection for July 22.

Read the official aa.org reflection for July 22 →
Recovery Einstein

For someone in recovery, 'the good and the bad' means learning to accept life on life's terms, without the buffer of alcohol. The Big Book teaches that we can no longer run from our problems—we must face both the pleasant and painful moments with equanimity. On page 85, it says we have a 'daily reprieve' from alcoholism, but this reprieve depends on our spiritual condition, which requires us to accept both the joys and sorrows of each day. The Promises on pages 83-84 assure us that we will 'not regret the past nor wish to shut the door on it'—meaning even the bad experiences have value in our recovery. The key is to stop judging every event as 'good' or 'bad' and instead see each as a lesson or an opportunity for growth. As page 68 says, 'We asked God to help us show them the same tolerance, pity, and patience that we would cheerfully give a sick friend.' This includes ourselves—we must be patient with our own imperfections and the ups and downs of daily life.

— Chapter 6, pages 83-86
Something to sit with

Think about the last 'bad' thing that happened to you this week. How did your old thinking label it as bad, and how might the Big Book's perspective on acceptance change your reaction?

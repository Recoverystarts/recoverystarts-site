The reading room

Start here

Everything on Recovery Starts that's meant to be read, on one page. That's the Twelve Steps, Traditions and Concepts, the story of how A.A. began, the Big Book page by page, answers to the questions people ask about A.A., and a reading for every day of the year.

Each page is written from A.A.'s own literature and names the page you can check it against. Every page is free, with no account and no sign-up. Recovery Starts is an independent project, not affiliated with Alcoholics Anonymous World Services. If you're looking for a meeting tonight, the meetings map and online meetings are the place to start.

The Twelve Steps

The Twelve Steps are A.A.'s program of recovery, first printed in the book Alcoholics Anonymous (the Big Book) in April 1939, in the chapter "How It Works" on pages 59–60. Our page sets out each Step and where the Big Book explains how to take it.

  • The 12 Steps in the Big Book — Page-by-Page

The Twelve Traditions

The Traditions are the principles A.A. groups adopted in 1950 to keep the fellowship together and unowned. The Big Book prints the long form in Appendix I (pp. 563–566), and our page sets out what each one actually says. The history page follows how they were worked out from 1937 to 1950, from the letters and the sources.

  • The 12 Traditions of A.A. — What They Actually Say (Long Form, pp. 563–566)
  • How the 12 Traditions Came About (1937–1950): The History, From the Sources

The Twelve Concepts for World Service

The Concepts, adopted by the General Service Conference in 1962, describe how A.A.'s service structure works. The Big Book's Appendix VII carries only their short form; our page has both forms in full.

  • The Twelve Concepts for World Service — Both Forms, In Full

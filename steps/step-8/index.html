Step 8 of 12 — Brotherly Love

Step 8: The Amends List

Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all.

Step 8 says: "Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all." It stands eighth among the Twelve Steps on page 59 of the Big Book, and its working instructions live in Chapter 6, Into Action, beginning on page 76. This is a pencil-and-paper Step. Nobody gets called, nobody gets visited, and no doors get knocked on yet. We make a list, and we become willing. That is the whole assignment, and the Big Book builds a surprising amount of mercy into it.

What Step 8 Says and Where It Lives in the Big Book

Right after the Seventh Step Prayer on page 76, the book turns to the amends Steps with a reminder that "Faith without works is dead" (page 76). Then it says something that surprises many of us. Speaking of Steps Eight and Nine together, the book states, "We have a list of all persons we have harmed and to whom we are willing to make amends. We made it when we took inventory" (page 76). In other words, the Big Book does not treat Step 8 as a brand-new project. The Fourth Step inventory on pages 64 to 71 already surfaced the names. The people in our resentment column, the people tangled up in our fears, and the people we hurt in our sex conduct review are sitting right there in our own handwriting.

How the Big Book Instructs Us to Work It

Working Step 8 has two motions. First, we make the list. We go back through our inventory and write down every person we actually harmed. The posture is the one the book taught us on page 67, where we were told, "Putting out of our minds the wrongs others had done, we resolutely looked for our own mistakes." That same discipline builds the amends list. A name goes on the list because we did damage, not because the other person was innocent. Many of the people we harmed also harmed us, and the book asks us to set that aside for now. We look for real harm: money owed, trust broken, promises abandoned, reputations dented, families frightened.

Second, we become willing. The book knows some names will stick in our throat, and it gives plain instructions for exactly that moment: "If we haven't the will to do this, we ask until it comes" (page 76). Willingness is not something we manufacture by gritting our teeth. We pray for it, and we keep praying until it arrives. The book also reminds us on the same page that we agreed at the beginning to go to any lengths for victory over alcohol. The list is one of those lengths.

The Fear That Stops Us Here

Let's be honest about why so many of us stall at Step 8. The moment the list stops being an idea and becomes actual names, fear shows up. We are afraid of certain faces. We are afraid of what admitting harm might cost us in money, in pride, or in relationships we only barely repaired. And underneath it all is the old argument that half the people on the list hurt us worse than we ever hurt them. The Big Book does not scold us for any of this. It quietly separates the list from the action. Step 8 asks for willingness only; Step 9 will handle the doing, with careful guidance and timing of its own. Today we need nothing but paper, honesty, and the prayer on page 76 for willingness we do not yet have. That much we can do.

What Changes, and Where This Leads

Something shifts when the wreckage of our past moves out of the fog and onto a single sheet of paper. The book describes the amends process as an attempt to sweep away the debris that piled up while we were living on self-will and running the show ourselves (page 76). A written list makes that debris finite. It has edges. It can be worked. Guilt that used to circle us at night becomes a set of names we are preparing, with God's help, to face. And the purpose behind it is bigger than tidying our own history. The book says, "Our real purpose is to fit ourselves to be of maximum service to God and the people about us" (page 77). The list is how we get fit.

The Promises that follow the amends work include this one: "We will not regret the past nor wish to shut the door on it" (page 83). That freedom starts with the list we write today. When the names are down and the willingness has come, even for the hard ones, we are ready for Step 9, where we go to the people we have harmed and set things right, directly wherever possible, except when doing so would injure them or others. The list is the map. Step 9 is the journey.

— Alcoholics Anonymous (Big Book), 4th Edition: page 59 (Step Eight as listed in How It Works); pages 76-77 (Into Action, working instructions for Steps Eight and Nine, sweeping away the debris, maximum service); pages 64-71 (the Fourth Step inventory that supplies the names for the list); page 67 (putting aside the wrongs of others to find our own mistakes); page 83 (the Promises that follow the amends work).

Quick answers

What is the difference between Step 8 and Step 9 in AA?

Step 8 is the list and the willingness; Step 9 is the action. The Big Book treats them together starting on page 76, but Step 8 asks only that we write down everyone we harmed and become willing to make amends. No one is contacted until Step 9, which carries its own guidance about timing and about not injuring others.

What if I'm not willing to make amends to someone on my Step 8 list?

The Big Book expects this and answers it directly: 'If we haven't the will to do this, we ask until it comes' (page 76). We put the name on the list anyway and pray for willingness rather than forcing it. Willingness often arrives gradually, one name at a time, and nothing in Step 8 requires us to act before it does.
